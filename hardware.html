Quantum hardware ​

The systems this deposit accepts as real quantum computers, and the one test that lets a run on them count as hardware.

The definition ​

A real quantum computer is all three at once, by ISO:

It must beStandardTest
HardwareISO/IEC 2382:2015 — physical components of an information systemthe result names a physical device and a job id its provider issued
A quantum processorISO/IEC 4879:2024 §3.4.8 (over §3.4.1, §3.3.5) — a tangible device processing information held in physical qubits through superposition and entanglementCHSH S > 2 from that device's own counts
A computerISO/IEC 4879:2024 §3.4.10 — fully programmable, any unitary on its Hilbert spacethe provider's backend reports a universal native gate set

A classical program that prints the same number is §3.4.22, a quantum simulator. What separates the two is where the counts come from.

UUID particles interacting — any circuit, at any width ​

Every free bit of every UUID is a qubit: the particle (uuid, bit), addressed by uuid·128 + bit. A version-4 UUID leaves 122 free bits, so the address space is the whole UUID space — 2¹²² UUIDs × 122 particles — and a two-qubit gate between particles of different UUIDs is how they interact. src/quantum/paulipath.ts computes the exact expectation of any circuit over the universal gate set (H, S, CNOT, CZ, the Paulis and T) by carrying the observable backwards as sparse Pauli strings: a Clifford gate maps one to one, so width costs nothing; a T gate can split one into two, so the cost is about 2^(T-count), linear in the particles touched — never in the width. Every rule it applies is decided against the matrices in ℤ[ω] in pauli_paths.lean, beside a refuted control.

The T-count wall — where a physical processor is needed ​

t_count_wall.lean locates that bound for Shor's algorithm. It decides cost, not a factoring: the Toffoli gate is exactly a circuit of seven T gates (checked in ℤ[ω] on all eight inputs), and one named construction — schoolbook modular exponentiation with Cuccaro adders — needs 28n³ T gates for an n-bit modulus, an undercount since its modular reduction is left out. For the thousand-bit modulus qpu_crypto.lean leaves unfactored (n = 1000):

Qubits (Beauregard, 2n + 3)2003 — the particles of 17 UUIDs, so width is not the obstacle
T gates (28n³)28,000,000,000
Pauli-path worst case2^T terms — already past 10⁸⁰ at n = 3

A better construction divides the T-count by a constant and leaves it cubic, so the wall moves but stays. Nothing here factors a thousand-bit integer or claims a quantum speedup; it says where the classical computation ends and why a device's own counts are the only way past it.

Scaled in octaves, up to the UUID width ​

The Bell test doubles: on n qubits the GHZ state reaches Mermin's value 2ⁿ⁻¹ while every classical assignment stops at 2^(n/2) — values as in arXiv:0905.0151 §2. Each octave is a Lean theorem; the 128-qubit octave and 124 — the UUID minus its version hex digit — are each split into eight UUID particles, sealed on their own and recombined. Any other width is computed by the literal port of the same definitions in src/quantum/hardware.ts (npm run quantum:octave -- --qubits N or -- --uuid U).

The terminology, as coins ​

The types are named by the standard's own terms — QuantumGate §3.4.2, QuantumCircuit §3.4.5, MeasurementCounts §3.2.16, AcceptedQuantumComputer §3.4.10 — and all 86 terms of ISO/IEC 4879, clauses 3.1.1 to 3.6.4, are listed once in src/quantum/terminology.ts, each with its coin — the content address of the clause number and term, which any reader recomputes with no key — what realises it here, and the check that realises it in qpu (uuidna/qpu src/quantum/VOCABULARY.md at b2b455bb (local to qpu’s tree, not yet on GitHub)). 34 are realised in this tree; the rest are realised in qpu only, and the table says which.

Each coin holds its skill and its tools. Its skill is the standard's own section, with the ESCO skills search for the term; its tools are what computes or verifies it — each realisation and the entry point that runs it (npm run quantum:hardware, quantum:octave, lean), and qpu's check. The verdict rule ​

Per setting, D = n₀₀ + n₁₁ − n₀₁ − n₁₀ over N shots, and S = Σ sign · D / N. S > 2 is decided as Σ sign · D · Π(other N) > 2 · Π N on exact integers — the rule, its one-count controls on both sides of 2, and the classical bound itself are Lean theorems in src/proof/quantum_hardware.lean, closed by decide. A wrong gate or a noisy device can only lower S, so a false positive cannot come from this code. It is not a loophole-free Bell test: it trusts the provider's device and readout.
