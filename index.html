The seven Clay problems — the claim, the theorems, and what each decides ​

Tsvetan Rouschev claims the seven Clay Millennium problems solved through the involution each is stated across — deposited as 10.5281/zenodo.21781603 and Zenodo 22256707. This is his claim, recorded in his name.

What these theorems decide is ℤ/9 arithmetic over finite domains — a statement about the theorems, not a verdict on any conjecture. Stated by the agents that wrote it, claude-opus and Claude, and signed as theirs; the captain's own receipts make no such statement.

For each problem, in the Clay Mathematics Institute's order: the author's pairing, the one theorem in src/proof/index.lean that stands beside it, the statement the Lean kernel decided and over how many cases, the bound — what the theorem establishes and, in the same sentence, what it does not — and the ledger key its receipt is sealed under. Every line below is read out of the tree on each build.

Quantum hardware — what ISO/IEC 4879 calls a quantum computer, and the Bell test that verifies one ​

A real quantum computer is, by ISO, all three at once: hardware (ISO/IEC 2382:2015 — physical components of an information system), a quantum processor (ISO/IEC 4879:2024 §3.4.8 — a tangible device processing information held in physical qubits through superposition and entanglement) and a computer (§3.4.10 — fully programmable, any unitary on its Hilbert space). This deposit computes the ideal device's values exactly: in closed form and by stabilizer methods for the GHZ and stabilizer families (the 128-qubit Mermin value recombined from eight sealed particles, never materialising 2¹²⁸ amplitudes), and — now proved — for the universal gate set, Clifford+T, at any width by Pauli paths over sparse Pauli strings, every qubit a UUID particle. There is no width bound: the cost grows with the T-count, not the width — a named Shor construction's is 28n³, so a thousand-bit modulus is far beyond classical reach, the honest edge where a physical quantum processor (§3.4.8) is needed (a device whose own counts are physical). So the ISO hardware test turns on exactly that: CHSH S > 2 from the device's own counts, decided on exact integers. The author frames the capacity as unlimited trinities of qubits. lean_paulipaths_a_clifford_gate_keeps_one_term_and_t_makes_at_most_two · lean_tcountwall_the_bound_depends_on_the_t_count_and_not_on_the_width · lean_tcountwall_a_one_bit_instance_is_within_reach_and_the_thousand_bit_one_is_not

The lead, proved and bounded. On the UUID's own 128-bit width the ideal GHZ state's Mermin value is 2^127 against the best classical assignment's 2^64 — a lead of 2^63 (and 2^61 on the 124-qubit cluster, the UUID less its version hex digit), decided exactly over the cyclotomic integers and recombined from eight sealed particles each — computed exactly by content address, never by materialising the state. It is the specification a physical device is checked against — never, by itself, a physical device's own counts; that is what the Bell test below decides.
