\begin{abstract}
We study a finite-arithmetic framework on the ring $\mathbb{Z}/9$ (the ``vortex'')
and the group $(\mathbb{Z}/7)^{*}$ (the seven-ray Pliska ``rosette''), from an
observation standpoint: each statement is computed, not asserted. Division by zero
is treated as a change of domain ($n/0 := n^{-1} \bmod 9$ on units; $0 \leftrightarrow
\infty$ on the Riemann sphere); inversion and the ten's-complement reflection
$10-d$ are involutions; a $7=6+1$ bijection maps the rosette (six units $+$ center)
onto the Clay set (six open problems $+$ Poincar\'e). A mechanical entailment test
reports \textbf{0/7}. \textbf{Observed conclusion:} the framework reflects/maps the
Millennium Problems into one algebraic structure; it does \emph{not} solve them,
and it is not a physical theory. It is a proof of concept and a classical simulator.
All results recompute from source (TypeScript; formalized in Lean~4).
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope}
This is not a resolution of the Clay Millennium Problems. The earlier
``$\exists\,\alpha,\ \top \wedge 1^2=1 \wedge 1=1$'' statements are tautologies:
true in a world where the conjecture is false, hence entailing nothing (0/7). What
is exact here is the $\mathbb{Z}/9$ / $(\mathbb{Z}/7)^{*}$ algebra, the reflections
and folds, and the geometry (cube $\to$ hexagon $\to$ Seed/Flower/Fruit/Metatron).

\section{Results (computed)}
\begin{itemize}
\item Units $(\mathbb{Z}/9)^{*}=\{1,2,4,5,7,8\}$; nilradical $\{0,3,6,9\}$ with
      $3^2\equiv 6^2\equiv 0$; doubling $\langle 2\rangle$ is $C_6$.
\item $10-d$ is an involution, fixed digit $5$; multiplicative inverses
      $2\leftrightarrow5$, $4\leftrightarrow7$.
\item $(\mathbb{Z}/7)^{*}\cong C_6 \cong (\mathbb{Z}/9)^{*}$; generator
      $3\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 7)\leftrightarrow 2\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 9)$.
\item Shell-model magic numbers $2,8,20,28,50,82,126$ (oscillator + spin-orbit);
      the $10\cdot 2^{k}$ ladder hits only the oscillator closures $20,40$.
\item Proton/electron ratio: fit $108\cdot 17 = 1836$ vs measured $1836.15267343$;
      $1836 \neq 1836.1527$ (curve-fit, not prediction).
\end{itemize}
