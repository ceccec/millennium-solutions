## `capacity.lean`

<p class="paper-src"><code>src/iso/22739-ledger/capacity.lean</code> · namespace <code>Capacity</code> · 16 theorems · wing <code>the address</code></p>

WHY THIS FILE EXISTS.

src/iso/iec-9834-8-identifiers/imprint.lean already decides the bit count: `FREE.length = 122 ∧ RESERVED.length = 6 ∧
FREE.length + RESERVED.length = 128`. That is sealed and it is honest.

WHAT WAS NOT DECIDED ANYWHERE IS WHAT FOLLOWS FROM IT. The number a reader actually quotes is not the bit
count — it is the CAPACITY (how many addresses) and the COLLISION EXPONENT (how hard a forgery is), and
neither had a theorem. src/iso/iec-9834-8-identifiers/address.lean states 122 correctly in a COMMENT; the ledger, which is
what the MCP server, the site and the trial all consume, carried nothing. A gap where the honest figure
should be does not stay empty: prose fills it with the container size, 2^128, because 128 is the number
printed on the tin.

The gap is exactly a factor of 64 — one doubling per reserved bit — and on the birthday bound it is a
factor of 8. Those are not rounding. A claim of 2^128 addresses overstates the space by 64×, and 2^64
overstates the work a collision search faces by 8×.

THIS IS A HEAL, NOT A WITHDRAWAL. Nothing above is being taken back: the container really is 128 bits and
imprint.lean's count really is 122. What is added is the cross formula between them, so the honest figure
and the nominal one sit in one place where they cannot be quoted apart. After this, writing 2^128 as the
capacity contradicts a sealed theorem rather than merely disagreeing with a comment.

No axioms, no Mathlib, no sorry.

<p class="thm-remark">── 4 · THE BIRTHDAY BOUND HALVES THE EXPONENT, AND HALVES THE HONEST ONE ───────────────────────────────── A collision is expected near 2^(n/2). Taken on the container that is 2^64; taken on what is actually free it is 2^61 — the number a forgery attempt really faces. AND THE HALVED WORLD LOSES THE CEILING OF HALF THE RESERVATION, NOT HALF OF IT. Stating the drop as r/2 would be wrong on every odd reservation, because the floor division that halves the exponent cannot spend half a bit: the exponent lost is (r+1)/2. So an odd reservation costs a forger exactly what the next even one costs, and 6 and 5 are the same bound. This is the arithmetic the typed 61 was resting on.</p>

<pre class="thm-statement"><code>(List.range (container / 4 + 1)).all (fun n =&gt; 4 * n / 4 == n &amp;&amp; (n - 2 + 2 == n) == decide (2 ≤ n)) ∧ container / 4 = 32 ∧ version / 4 = 1 ∧ container / 4 - (version / 4 + 1) = 30</code></pre>
<details class="thm-tex"><summary>LaTeX source</summary><pre class="thm-latex"><code>\forall n \in \{0,\dots,\frac{\mathrm{container}}{4} + 1-1\},\; \frac{4 \cdot n}{4} = n \land n - 2 + 2 = n = \mathrm{decide}\mathopen{}\left(2 \le n\right) \land \frac{\mathrm{container}}{4} = 32 \land \frac{\mathrm{version}}{4} = 1 \land \frac{\mathrm{container}}{4} - \frac{\mathrm{version}}{4} + 1 = 30</code></pre></details>
</div>
<p class="thm-remark">read as hex handles: 32 nibbles, less the one the version fills and the one the variant touches = 30 free. AS A LAW: at EVERY register of n whole nibbles up to the container's 32, a width of 4n bits reads back as n handles, and the two reserved handles subtract and restore (n − 2 + 2 = n) exactly when there are two to take (2 ≤ n) — below that the subtraction truncates and nothing is restored. 128 bits is the instance n = 32.</p>
<p class="thm-proof"><em>Proof.</em> <code>by decide</code> — by evaluation; no domain is walked. <span class="qed">□</span></p>
