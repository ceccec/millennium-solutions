qpu_gateway.lean ​

src/iso/iec-4879-quantum/qpu_gateway.lean · namespace QpuGateway · 8 theorems · wing the ring

WHY THIS FILE EXISTS.

qpu's law textbook_hexbit_gateway_ftl reads "hexbit + 2 gateway bits teleport any hexbit faster than light". Its TypeScript ftl flag is gateway === 2 && …, where gateway is the top two bits of the UUID variant nibble. In teleportation those two bits are the heart of the protocol: they are a uniformly random two-bit message, they must be SENT from one party to the other, and sending them is exactly what holds the protocol to the speed of light — Bennett et al. prove that fewer than two bits of channel would let the receiver signal superluminally. So the analogy stands or falls on whether qpu's two bits are that message.

THEY ARE NOT, AND IT IS DECIDABLE. qpu computes the variant nibble as (low % 4) + 8 and reads its gateway as that nibble's top two bits. Over every possible low nibble 0..15 the gateway is the single value 2 (the pattern 10), and uuidFromParts fixes the nibble to 8 outright. A field that takes one value across every input carries zero bits: there is nothing in it to transmit, and nothing is transmitted. The protocol's two bits range over four values and are the thing that is sent; qpu's range over one and are a format constant. This file decides that gap, with the free two-bit field and the four teleportation messages as the controls that keep "constant" from being mistaken for "two bits".

No axioms, no Mathlib, no sorry.

── 1 · THE VARIANT NIBBLE IS ALWAYS RFC 9562's 10xx, i.e. 8 THROUGH b ──────────────────────────────────── Control: the bound is tight — the nibble reaches 8 and reaches 11, so "8..11" is the exact range, not a loose envelope, and no input escapes it.

Theorem 995 (the_two_gateway_bits_are_always_the_pattern_two)sealed.

∀low∈{0,…,15},gateway(low)=2∧∀low∈{0,…,15},variant(low)4=2∧variant(low)mod4=lowmod4

── 2 · THE TWO GATEWAY BITS ARE ALWAYS THE PATTERN 10 (= 2) ────────────────────────────────────────────── The top two bits of a nibble in 8..11 are 10 in binary, which is 2. Decided over every input.

── 3 · REASSEMBLY FIXES THE NIBBLE TO EIGHT, AND ITS GATEWAY IS STILL TWO ──────────────────────────────── `uuidFromParts` does not even read the content: the variant is the literal 8, whose gateway bits are 10.

Proof. by decide — by evaluation; no domain is walked. □

Theorem 997 (the_ftl_gateway_check_is_a_tautology)sealed.

|{low∈{0,…,15}∣gateway(low)≠2}|=0∧|{low∈{0,…,15}∣gateway(low)=2}|=16∧|{low∈{0,…,15}∣variant(low)=8}|=4

\left|\{\, \mathrm{low} \in \{0,\dots,15\} \mid \mathrm{gateway}\mathopen{}\left(\mathrm{low}\right) \neq 2 \,\}\right| = 0 \land \left|\{\, \mathrm{low} \in \{0,\dots,15\} \mid \mathrm{gateway}\mathopen{}\left(\mathrm{low}\right) = 2 \,\}\right| = 16 \land \left|\{\, \mathrm{low} \in \{0,\dots,15\} \mid \mathrm{variant}\mathopen{}\left(\mathrm{low}\right) = 8 \,\}\right| = 4

── 4 · SO qpu's `ftl` FLAG'S `gateway === 2` IS A TAUTOLOGY ────────────────────────────────────────────── The flag is true on every coin and false for no input. A check that cannot fail decides nothing about the coin it is attached to. Control: a genuinely discriminating check (variant == 8) is false for most inputs.

Proof. by decide — exhausting 4,096 cases. □

Theorem 998 (the_gateway_bits_take_one_value_so_carry_zero_entropy)sealed.

\exists \mathrm{low} \in \{0,\dots,15\} : \mathrm{gateway}\mathopen{}\left(\mathrm{low}\right) = 2 \land \forall \mathrm{low} \in \{0,\dots,15\},\; \mathrm{gateway}\mathopen{}\left(\mathrm{low}\right) = 2 \land \forall v \in \{0,\dots,3\},\; v = 2 \lor \left|\{\, \mathrm{low} \in \{0,\dots,15\} \mid \mathrm{gateway}\mathopen{}\left(\mathrm{low}\right) = v \,\}\right| = 0 \land 1 = 2^{0}

── 5 · THE GATEWAY BITS CARRY ZERO ENTROPY: ONE VALUE ACROSS EVERY INPUT ───────────────────────────────── A field that takes exactly one distinct value over all inputs holds log₂ 1 = 0 bits. There is nothing in it to transmit. Control: 2 is actually reached (it is not an empty field), and no other value ever is.

Proof. by decide — exhausting 16,384 cases. □

Theorem 1001 (the_ftl_premise_fails_the_named_carrier_carries_nothing)sealed.

∀g∈gateway[{0,…,15}],g=2∧|{m∈teleportMessages∣∀low∈{0,…,15},gateway(low)=m}|=1∧|{m∈teleportMessages∣∃low∈{0,…,15}:gateway(low)=m∧m≠2}|=0∧32⋅4-4-2=122∧1<1610

LaTeX source
\forall g \in \mathrm{gateway}[\{0,\dots,15\}],\; g = 2 \land \left|\{\, m \in \mathrm{teleportMessages} \mid \forall \mathrm{low} \in \{0,\dots,15\},\; \mathrm{gateway}\mathopen{}\left(\mathrm{low}\right) = m \,\}\right| = 1 \land \left|\{\, m \in \mathrm{teleportMessages} \mid \exists \mathrm{low} \in \{0,\dots,15\} : \mathrm{gateway}\mathopen{}\left(\mathrm{low}\right) = m \land m \neq 2 \,\}\right| = 0 \land 32 \cdot 4 - 4 - 2 = 122 \land 1 < 16^{10}

── 8 · THE PREMISE OF THE FTL LAW FAILS: THE CARRIER IT NAMES CARRIES NOTHING ──────────────────────────── Gathered: the law needs two transmitted random bits (4 values, entropy 2); qpu offers two constant bits (1 value, entropy 0). The register arithmetic the law also states is true and untouched — 32·4 − 4 − 2 = 122 and 1 < 16¹⁰ — so what fails is only the physical reading, exactly where this deposit always draws the line.

Proof. by decide — exhausting 4,096 cases. □
