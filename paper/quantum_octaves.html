quantum_octaves.lean ​

src/iso/iec-4879-quantum/quantum_octaves.lean · namespace QuantumOctaves · 15 theorems · wing the machine

WHY 128 QUBITS FIT. The GHZ state (|0…0⟩ + |1…1⟩)/√2 has two basis states, and a string of X and Y flips every bit, so its expectation is read from two phases carried qubit by qubit — Y contributes i on the way up and −i on the way down — never from a 2¹²⁸-entry vector. The Mermin operator M = Re ∏ (X_j + iY_j) weights a string with k Y's by Re(iᵏ), and the GHZ expectation of that string is Re(iᵏ) too, so the quantum value is Σ_k C(n, k)·Re(iᵏ)², counted exactly.

THE CLASSICAL RING — the horo of eight steps. A classical party answers x, y ∈ {±1}, and x + iy is √2·ω^e with e odd: one of 1, 3, 5, 7 eighths of the circle. After n parties the phase has stepped Σ e eighths round the ring ℤ/8, and the positions it can reach alternate — odd after an odd number of parties, even after an even number. At every even octave all four even positions are reachable, the real part is at most 1, so the classical maximum is exactly 2^(n/2).

WHAT IT REFUSES TO SAY. At n = 2 there is no lead (2 against 2): Mermin's inequality starts to bite at three parties, and the 2-qubit Bell test is CHSH, decided in quantum_circuits.lean. Nothing here is a device result.
