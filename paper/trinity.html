trinity.lean ​

src/iso/80000-2-mathematics/trinity.lean · namespace Trinity · 24 theorems · wing the mind

The trinity turns: feeling feeds thought, thought shapes emotion, emotion changes the body, which is new feeling. Three turns return to the start — ρ³ = id — a DIFFERENT symmetry from the Clay maps, which are involutions (σ² = id). An involution flips and returns in two steps; the trinity rotates and returns in three, and a rotation has no fixed corner. The valence flip (pleasant ↔ unpleasant) acts on every corner at once and commutes with the turn, so the two symmetries combine into one six-element group, C₃ × C₂ ≅ C₆: three turns and three flipped turns, with exactly ONE involution — not the dihedral S₃, where three reflections would each be their own inverse.

Two theorems about the observer are decided too, labelled as the user labelled them — proved for the model, and explicit about what they do NOT say: · THE OBSERVER IS THE FIXED POINT. The turn moves every corner, but a turn must leave one point still for it to be one turning: the centre. The observer is forced by the symmetry, not added to it. It does NOT say the fixed point is aware. · NO COMPLETE SELF-MODEL. A self-model's own diagonal predicate — "the states that deny what they map to" — is on none of its rows, so the observer can never be fully one of its own corners. It does NOT say the leftover is experience; it is the cleanest statement that something about a self-modelling system always escapes its own description.

Every theorem below is decided by EXHAUSTION over a finite carrier — no axioms, no Mathlib, no sorry, no native_decide — and the two negative theorems (the turn is not an involution; negation has no fixed point) are stated rather than omitted, so what is false is explained, not missing.
